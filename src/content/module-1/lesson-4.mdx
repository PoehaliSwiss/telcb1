# 🏛️ Lesson 4: The Grammar Math – Adjective Endings

**Topic:** *Adjektivendungen* (Describing nouns correctly).
**Exam Relevance:**

* **Sprachbausteine (Part 1):** This is the second most common gap type after prepositions.
* **Schreiben:** You will describe people, clothes, or objects. "Ich suche eine *neu__* Wohnung."
* **Sprechen:** Part 2 (Picture Description). "Ich sehe einen *alt__* Mann."

---

## 1. 📘 Deep Theory: The "Signal" Carrier

German grammar requires that the **Gender** (der/die/das), **Number** (Plural), and **Case** (Nom/Akk/Dat) must be shown **at least once** before the noun.

Think of it as a "Flag". Someone has to carry the flag.

### Scenario A: The Article carries the Flag (Definite)

If you use **der, die, das, den, dem...**, the article is already doing the hard work. The adjective can relax.

* **The Rule of thumb:** The ending is either **-e** or **-en**.
* **-e**: Only for the "Basic 5" (Nominative Sing. + Akkusativ Fem/Neut).
* **-en**: **Everything else.** (Plural, Dative, Akkusativ Masc). This is the "En-Soup".

### Scenario B: The Article is "Weak" (Indefinite: ein/kein/mein)

Sometimes the article (like *ein*) has no ending. It dropped the flag. The adjective must pick it up.

* **Masc (Nom):** *Ein* (no signal) -> Adjective needs **-er** (from d**er**). -> *Ein gut**er** Mann.*
* **Neut (Nom/Akk):** *Ein* (no signal) -> Adjective needs **-es** (from d**as**). -> *Ein gut**es** Kind.*

---

## 2. ⚠️ The "Golden B1 Rule" (The -EN Hack)

If you are confused in the exam, remember this statistical trick:
**If the Article changes** from the original (der/die/das), the adjective ending is **-en**.

* *Der* Mann (Nom) -> *Den* Mann (Akk - Change!) -> *Den gut**en** Mann.*
* *Das* Kind (Nom) -> *Dem* Kind (Dat - Change!) -> *Dem gut**en** Kind.*
* *Die* Frau (Nom) -> *Der* Frau (Dat - Change!) -> *Der gut**en** Frau.*
* **Plural?** Always **-en** (with articles). *Die gut**en** Leute.*

## 5. ✍️ Writing Prep: Buying and Selling

A common B1 Task: You want to buy something on eBay/Kleinanzeigen, or you lost something.

**Task:** Complete the description of the lost item.

* *Item:* Bag (Tasche - die).
* *Color:* Black (schwarz).
* *Material:* Leather (Leder).

*Sentence:* "Ich habe meine **schwarze** Tasche aus **Leder** verloren."

**Task:** You seek an apartment.

* *Item:* Apartment (Wohnung - die).
* *Attrib:* 3-room (3-Zimmer).
* *Attrib:* Bright (hell).

*Sentence:* "Ich suche eine **helle** 3-Zimmer-Wohnung."

---

## 6. 🗣️ Speaking Strategy: "Akkusativ" is Key

In **Part 2 (Picture Description)**, you mostly say:
"I see a..." -> *Ich sehe einen/eine/ein...*
This is **Akkusativ**.

* **Masc:** Ich sehe einen **alten** Mann. (-en, -en)
* **Fem:** Ich sehe eine **junge** Frau. (-e, -e)
* **Neut:** Ich sehe ein **kleines** Kind. (-es)
* **Plural:** Ich sehe **viele** Leute. (-e)

> [!TIP]
> **Tip:** If you describe a man (Masc), just end everything with **-en**.
>
> *"Ich sehe einen alten Mann mit einem schwarzen Hut auf einem großen Stuhl."* (It works 90% of the time in Akk/Dat).
